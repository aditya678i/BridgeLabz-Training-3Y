Reading on Screens

A short note on why typography still matters online.

Good typography on the web is mostly invisible. Readers rarely notice correct line spacing or comfortable letter tracking — they simply keep reading without friction. It is bad typography that gets noticed, usually as fatigue after a few paragraphs.

Three variables matter more than most others: font size, line height, and measure — the width of a line of text. A line that is too wide makes it hard for the eye to find its way back to the start of the next line.

This paragraph switches to a serif typeface at a slightly larger size and heavier weight, just to show how family, size, and weight can shift the tone of a page without changing a single word.

Most of the time, though, the safest choice is a plain system font at a modest size with generous line-height — readable first, distinctive second.
